# Historical scientific research notes

This file preserves the source review for the first version of the learning atlas. The old ABO/reagent exercise and generated microscope scenes described below were removed from the microscope page. The current microscope displays bundled photographs directly; see [scientific-sources.md](scientific-sources.md) and [specimen-photo-credits.md](specimen-photo-credits.md) for the current implementation.

## ABO/Rh forward typing

For standard educational phenotypes, Anti-A detects A antigen, Anti-B detects B antigen, and Anti-D detects D antigen. The eight-group outcome matrix in the app uses these independent results. A reagent-antibody link to its matching red-cell antigen can produce visible agglutination. A non-agglutinating result means that corresponding antigen was not detected under the stated conditions. Forward typing alone is not compatibility testing. Weak/partial D variants, weak expression, technical factors, antibody screening, and crossmatching remain outside the simulation.

Sources: Frontiers in Medicine, “Blood Group Testing” (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/medicine/articles/10.3389/fmed.2022.827619/full); HRSA/OPTN, “Guidance for Addressing Blood Type Determination” (https://www.hrsa.gov/optn/professionals/resources/guidance/guidance-for-addressing-blood-type-determination); Canadian Blood Services, “Pre-transfusion testing” (https://professionaleducation.blood.ca/en/transfusion/clinical-guide/pre-transfusion-testing).

## Peripheral blood smear

Normal smear labels include erythrocytes as anucleate biconcave discs with central pallor, typically about 7–8 μm; platelets as small anucleate granulated bodies; segmented neutrophils; small lymphocytes with large dark nuclei and scant cytoplasm; and the source-supported morphology of monocytes, eosinophils, and basophils. A stained smear's appearance varies with preparation and stain, and the display is not diagnostic.

Sources: Yale Team-Based Learning, “Blood Smears Lab — Reading” (https://medcell.org/tbl/blood_smears/reading.php); Adewoyin & Nwogoh, “Peripheral Blood Film - A Review,” Annals of Ibadan Postgraduate Medicine (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4415389/).

## Onion epidermis

The specimen is the single-layer thin-walled inner bulb epidermis. Source-supported visible teaching labels are cell wall, cytoplasm, central vacuole, and nucleus. Stain and contrast affect visibility; routine light microscopy does not reliably show every internal membrane or organelle. The UI avoids unsourced fixed claims about polygonal shape or precise cell arrangement.

Sources: Iowa State University, “Plant Cells and Tissues” (https://iastate.pressbooks.pub/isudp-2025-205/chapter/plant-cells-and-tissues/); peer-reviewed Plant Cell Reports article on onion epidermis (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2778400/); Tidewater Community College, “Cells | Biology I Laboratory Manual” (https://courses.lumenlearning.com/biolabs1/chapter/cells/).

## Saccharomyces cerevisiae

The model depicts a unicellular eukaryotic fungus dividing by budding: a smaller daughter bud grows from a mother cell. Cells can be spherical or ovoid and their sizes vary. It does not represent universal biosafety; the app recommends authorized teaching cultures and institutional procedures.

Sources: “Budding Yeast for Budding Geneticists,” Genetics (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4012490/); “The cell morphological diversity of Saccharomycotina yeasts,” FEMS Yeast Research (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10804222/); ASM teaching-lab biosafety guidance (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3706168/).

## Generalized dicot leaf

Supported labels include cuticle, epidermis, palisade and spongy mesophyll, vascular bundle, xylem, phloem, stomata, and guard cells. Functions in the app are summarized at introductory level. Species, habitat, tissue thickness, and stomatal distribution vary, so the diagram is explicitly generalized.

Sources: LibreTexts Botany, “Internal Leaf Structure” (https://bio.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Botany/Botany_%28Ha_Morrow_and_Algiers%29/03%3A_Plant_Structure/03.04%3A_Leaves/3.4.02%3A_Internal_Leaf_Structure); University of Minnesota Libraries Publishing, “Inside Leaves” (https://open.lib.umn.edu/horticulture/chapter/5-1-inside-leaves/); OpenStax, “Leaves” (https://openstax.org/books/biology-ap-courses/pages/23-4-leaves).

## Human heart

The schema uses the usual adult flow sequence: venae cavae, right atrium, tricuspid valve, right ventricle, pulmonary valve, pulmonary arteries, lungs, pulmonary veins, left atrium, mitral valve, left ventricle, aortic valve, aorta, and body. It does not represent fetal circulation or pathology.

Sources: NHLBI/NIH, “How Blood Flows through the Heart” (https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart/blood-flow); NHLBI/NIH, “What the Heart Looks Like” (https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/heart/anatomy); UCSF Department of Surgery, “How the Heart Works” (https://surgery.ucsf.edu/condition/how-heart-works).

## Animal-cell schema

The introductory schematic labels plasma membrane, nucleus, nucleolus, mitochondria, rough/smooth ER, Golgi apparatus, ribosomes, and lysosomes. Functions are summarized from OpenStax. The model is neither to scale nor a universal inventory, and it does not represent plant-cell structures as normal animal-cell structures.

Sources: OpenStax Biology 2e, “Eukaryotic Cells” (https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/4-3-eukaryotic-cells); OpenStax Anatomy and Physiology 2e, “The Cytoplasm and Cellular Organelles” (https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/3-2-the-cytoplasm-and-cellular-organelles).

## Introductory lab apparatus

The schema distinguishes vessels for holding/mixing (beaker, Erlenmeyer flask, test tube) from measurement/delivery instruments (graduated cylinder, volumetric pipette), and includes a balance and burner. No accuracy numbers are claimed. The burner includes qualified safety guidance and is not a substitute for local rules.

Sources: Chemistry LibreTexts / Bethune-Cookman University, “Lab Equipment” (https://chem.libretexts.org/Courses/BethuneCookman_University/B-CU%3A_CHL-141_General_Chemistry_1_Lab/Labs/Laboratory_1%3A_Basic_Lab_Techniques/Lab_Equipment); Indiana University East, “Using Standard Laboratory Equipment” (https://iu.pressbooks.pub/iuegenchemlabs/front-matter/using-standard-laboratory-equipment/); USC Environmental Health and Safety, “Laboratory Burner Safety” (https://ehs.usc.edu/research/lab/laboratory-burner-safety/).
